The Beginning: I Thought I Knew SQL

When I first opened LeetCode’s SQL section, I was confident.

I had written queries before. I knew what SELECT, JOIN, and GROUP BY meant.

So how hard could it be?

I still remember that first problem — “Combine Two Tables.”

I typed out my query with full swagger, hit “Run,” and…

❌ Wrong Answer.

Wait, what?

I checked again. My logic looked right.

Then I noticed it — a NULL value in a column I didn’t consider.

That moment changed everything.

I realized SQL isn’t just about syntax — it’s about thinking like data.

The Middle: When Queries Started Talking Back

By the time I’d solved 50 questions, I’d developed an odd relationship with the database.

Every problem had a story.

  • A missing record was a mystery.
  • A tricky LEFT JOIN was a plot twist.
  • An INNER JOIN felt like two characters finally meeting their match.

Some problems looked easy — until you hit the real-world data traps:

Duplicate rows, missing joins, ambiguous columns, or filters that destroyed your entire logic.

That’s when I stopped coding queries blindly.

I started debugging with intent.

I didn’t just ask “What’s the query?” anymore — I asked “What’s the question behind the query?”

The (Honest) Disclaimer: Not Every Question

Before we go further, let’s be honest.

When I say I solved all the SQL questions — I mean all the non-premium ones 😅.

Because, let’s be real — not all of us can justify a LeetCode Premium subscription just to learn SQL.

But here’s the thing: even the non-premium questions are gold mines of learning.

They cover nearly every concept you’ll ever need in real-world analytics — from simple joins to window functions.

And they’re free. That’s enough to build mastery if you stay consistent.

The Turning Point: Thinking Like Data

After 100+ problems, something clicked.

I stopped seeing SQL as a “language.”

I started seeing it as a mindset.

Here’s what really changed:

  1. Joins became relationships, not operations.
    I could instantly visualize how tables connected — like social networks of data.
  2. Aggregation became storytelling.
    Every SUM, COUNT, and AVG was a line in a bigger narrative: What is this data trying to say?
  3. Optimization became art.
    I learned how indexes, subqueries, and execution plans worked — not because I had to, but because I wanted to make queries elegant.
  4. Real-world data ≠ textbook data.
    NULLs, duplicates, and messy structures taught me humility — data rarely behaves.

The Hard Lessons: Things Tutorials Never Told Me

Solving LeetCode SQL questions wasn’t just a grind — it was an emotional rollercoaster.

Here are some truths I wish I knew earlier:

  • DISTINCT is not a solution; it’s a band-aid.
    If you’re using it too often, you’re missing a relationship problem.
  • HAVING without GROUP BY is chaos.
    (Okay, technically possible, but almost never logical )
  • NULL will break your logic silently.
    Every time I saw a wrong output, 70% of the time — it was because of a NULL.
  • Order matters — even when you think it doesn’t.
    I learned to never assume the database “knows what I mean.”
  • SQL is philosophy disguised as code.
    It forces you to think about sets, relationships, and meaning. It teaches patience.

The Takeaway: SQL Changed How I Think

Today, when I look at a business problem, my brain doesn’t say:

“Let’s write a query.”

It says:

“Let’s design a story — one that starts with raw facts and ends with insight.”

LeetCode SQL didn’t just make me better at interviews.

It made me better at thinking in data — a skill that transcends any job title.

Now, when I mentor juniors, I tell them:

“SQL isn’t just how you talk to databases.
It’s how you listen to data.”

Bonus: My Personal SQL Hacks for LeetCode Success

If you’re just starting, or stuck halfway — here are the hacks that kept me sane:

  1. Draw before you query.
    Sketch table relationships. You’ll be shocked how visual SQL really is.
  2. Explain your query to a rubber duck.
    If you can’t explain it, you don’t understand it.
  3. Practice by topic, not by difficulty.
    Master joins first, then aggregations, then window functions.
  4. Focus on readability, not speed.
    Interviewers prefer clarity over premature optimization.
  5. Study execution plans once a week.
    They’re like a peek inside the database’s brain.
  6. Rewrite old problems from memory.
    That’s where real retention happens.

Final Reflection: The Real Gift

When I finished the last (non-premium 😉) SQL question on LeetCode, I didn’t feel like a “coder.”

I felt like an investigator — someone who could translate chaos into clarity.

SQL taught me something no syntax ever could:

Data has emotions too — you just have to know how to listen.
