Ever tried solving a dataset problem at 2 AM with your third cup of coffee in hand, only to realize the real problem was you?

Yeah. Same.

Hi. I’m Harshith. And this isn’t your usual “I built a dashboard, now I’m a data ninja” story.

This is a story about why I decided to pour my soul — and about 17 mugs of overly strong coffee — into building a full-blown Data + AI/ML project around something that actually matters: student depression.

🟡 Why: The Part Where I Cried First and Coded Later

We always start with the Golden Circle: Why → How → What

So, let’s begin with the “Why”.

I’ve seen it. You’ve seen it. Heck, you’ve probably felt it.

That crushing weight of assignments, rejections, fear of failure, not to mention what the hell am I doing with my life moments. As students, we’re often expected to “keep it together,” slap a smile on, and power through it all like caffeine-fueled productivity bots.

But behind those Instagram stories and neat bullet journals, there’s often a ton of unspoken emotional chaos.

I built this project because I’ve been there. Because friends of mine have been there. Because some didn’t make it out.

This project isn’t about grades or building a resume. It’s about trying to use what I’ve learned — data science, machine learning, and empathy — to say:

“We see you. We hear you. And we’re doing something about it.”

🛠️ How: Crying into a Dataset (and Making a Dashboard Too)

I found a dataset on Kaggle that felt too real to ignore.

It had details like:

  • Age
  • CGPA
  • Academic pressure
  • Sleep patterns (or the lack thereof, lol)
  • Dietary habits
  • Suicidal thoughts
  • Family history of mental illness

Each row? One student. One story.

What I Did:

  1. Built a Tableau Dashboard — To visualize depression trends, patterns, and what the data is trying to scream at us.
  2. Trained an AI/ML Model — To predict which students might be at risk, using features like CGPA, sleep hours, study pressure, etc.

The goal? Early intervention. Real insights. Real impact.

🔵 What: The Dashboard, the Model, and Me

Let’s get nerdy. But also kind.

This dashboard isn’t just charts and filters. It’s a lens into student mental health, broken into digestible (and beautiful) sections:

🔹 Overview: The Mental Health Snapshot

  • Depression % among students
  • Age/gender-wise breakdown
  • KPIs (Key Pain Indicators… wait no, wrong abbreviation)

🔹 Academic Pressure Cooker

  • Depression vs. CGPA (surprise: even toppers cry)
  • Study satisfaction vs. sadness (yes, it matters)
  • Pressure barometer visuals

🔹 Lifestyle & Sleep: The Midnight Scroll Saga

  • Sleep duration vs. depression (spoiler: sleep helps)
  • Work/study hour graphs
  • What students eat and how it affects their minds

🔹 Psychosocial Chaos

  • Financial stress levels
  • Family mental health history
  • Suicidal thoughts stat (aka the section that made me stop and breathe)

And of course, the ML model — a classifier that says:

“Hey, based on these factors, this student might need help. Let’s not ignore it.”

💬 Real Talk

This project taught me more than just data visualization and logistic regression.

It taught me that behind every datapoint is a person.

That sometimes, just asking “Are you okay?” matters more than an 85% accuracy score.

That coding for social good feels better than any Kaggle badge (but hey, those are nice too).

☕ One More Sip Before We Go

So here’s to the students crying quietly in library corners.

To the ones who smile but are screaming inside.

To the friends who stayed. And to the ones we lost too soon.

This project is for you.

And to whoever’s reading this — if you’re struggling, know that it’s okay to not be okay. But it’s not okay to stay silent.

There’s help. There’s hope. And yes, there’s coffee. Always coffee.
